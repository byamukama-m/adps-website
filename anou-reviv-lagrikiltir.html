Why this matters

Mauritius is losing touch with the land that feeds it

Food import dependency is rising, the farming population is ageing, and fewer young people see agriculture as a future worth choosing. This programme exists to change that trajectory, one classroom, one field visit, one harvest at a time.

75%of food consumed in Mauritius is imported

Program design

A five-year pathway, one grade at a time

Each grade spends one immersive week: five consecutive days, five different hosts across the agricultural value chain, building toward a deeper, more technical understanding of food systems.

Grade 7

Awareness & Introduction

Food security, local food systems and why agriculture matters to Mauritius.

Grade 8

Farming Basics

Crop production, livestock, soil health and sustainable growing practices.

Grade 9

Food Systems & Processing

How raw produce becomes packaged food, from factory floor to supply chain.

Grade 10

Markets & Agribusiness

Marketing, logistics, value addition and the commercial side of farming.

Grade 11

Innovation & Future

Climate-smart tech, precision farming, research and future career paths.

How partners take part

The "Adopt-a-Day" model

Organisations across the agricultural value chain, from farms and food processors to research bodies and public institutions, each host one full day of a student cohort's immersion week.

  • Contribute expertise, facilities and people, not just funding
  • ADPS co-designs the day's curriculum with you, tailored to your sector
  • Engage directly with the next generation of the industry's workforce
  • Strengthen your CSR commitments with measurable, reported impact

What it builds

Outcomes, tracked over time

The programme is designed for compounding impact, from a single training week to a resilient national food system.

Short term
  • Youth trained across diverse food production methods
  • Increased awareness of food security
  • School agricultural clubs established
Medium term
  • Youth-led agricultural initiatives
  • Rising interest in agricultural careers
  • Community engagement in food production
Long term
  • Increased local food production
  • Reduced import dependency
  • A revived, resilient agricultural sector
